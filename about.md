  <p class="eyebrow">About</p>

  <h1>
    Research & engineering background
  </h1>

  <p class="page-lead">
    I am a Ph.D. student in Mechatronics, Robotics, and Automation Engineering at Oregon State University and a graduate research assistant in the Manufacturing Process Control Laboratory.
  </p>

  <p>
    My work sits at the intersection of control systems, precision mechatronics, and advanced manufacturing, with a consistent workflow: characterize the physical system, build a useful model, design the control or adaptation strategy, implement it, and validate the result experimentally.
  </p>

</header>




<section class="about-section">

  <div class="about-section-heading">

    <p class="eyebrow">Background</p>

    <h2>Research background</h2>

  </div>


  <div class="prose-column">

    <p>
      During my master's research at Oregon State University, I worked on feed-modulated milling for chatter avoidance. The work combined machining-process kinematics, regenerative dynamics, stability analysis, adaptive control, feed-drive implementation, and experimental validation.
    </p>

    <p>
      Before joining Oregon State University, I completed my bachelor's degree in Mechanical Engineering at the University of Tehran. My undergraduate thesis focused on precision positioning of a planar voice-coil actuator, including experimental system identification, sensing and instrumentation, embedded control, and nonlinear friction characterization and modeling.
    </p>

    <p>
      Current research interests include precision motion and feed-drive systems, vibration and chatter mitigation, adaptive and learning-based control, experimental dynamics, and advanced manufacturing process control.
    </p>

  </div>

</section>
